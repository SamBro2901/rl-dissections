\chapter{Learning check details}
\label{app:learning}

This appendix documents the returns of the training runs, which the thesis uses as a sanity check only and never in a comparison (\zcref{sec:metric}).
\Zcref{sec:app-learn-def} defines the quantities, \zcref{sec:app-learn-curves} shows the return curves of all configurations, \zcref{sec:app-learn-hc} and \zcref{sec:app-learn-ant} give the learning check of HalfCheetah-v5 and Ant-v5, and \zcref{sec:app-learn-means} the means of all \num{56} configurations.
\Zcref{sec:app-learn-spearman} holds an optional extension of the rank-correlation analysis of \zcref{tab:spearman}, and \zcref{sec:app-learn-random} the placeholder for the return of a random policy.
All values were recomputed from the \texttt{training\_\allowbreak metrics.json} files of the \num{280} canonical runs.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Definitions}\label{sec:app-learn-def}

The returns are those of the training episodes, which run under the behaviour policy of the algorithm (a stochastic sample for SAC and MBPO, Gaussian action noise for TD3, noisy planning for TD-MPC2).
There are no separate evaluation episodes.

For a run with $n$ completed training episodes, the \emph{first \SI{10}{\percent}} and the \emph{last \SI{10}{\percent}} are its first and last $k=\max(1,\lfloor n/10\rfloor)$ training episodes.
The warm-up episodes (random actions) and the incomplete episode at the end of the run are not counted.
On HalfCheetah-v5, every episode ends after \num{1000} steps, so $n=\num{100}$ and $k=\num{10}$ in all \num{135} runs.
On Ant-v5, episodes end early when the robot leaves its healthy state; $n$ lies between \num{154} and \num{2556} per run, and $k$ between \num{15} and \num{255}.
The \emph{return} of the first (last) tenth of a run is the unweighted mean of the returns of its $k$ episodes.
The \emph{return per step} is the sum of the returns divided by the sum of the lengths of the same episodes, i.e.\ a ratio of sums and not a mean of per-episode ratios.
Values of a configuration are means over the five seeds, and the spread is the sample standard deviation ($n-1$) over the seeds.
